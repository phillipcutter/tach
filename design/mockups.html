Menu bar item

The item is a row of blocks you arrange yourself. By default it shows the tightest limit of each of the three subscriptions, then what the last 24 hours would have cost at API prices. It is white like a template image; colour is kept for warning and critical.

Dropdown panel

Direction A’s layout with B’s headline. The sentence about the limit most at risk comes first, then a ring pair per account, that account’s limits as linked groups, and models and machines on the same sheet.

The panel

Click a ring to switch account, and a limit to see its pace, projection and how many sessions the week still has room for. The range switcher works. About 900 pt tall with Claude open; on a shorter display the sheet scrolls.

Limit groups, every account open

The same component for all three accounts, so you can compare how it handles a pair with model limits, a single limit, and two separate budgets.

How to read a group

5-hour and weekThe 5-hour bar is a close-up of one slice of the week. The wedge joins what this session has used to the slice of the week it took, and the figure beside it says how much that is.
Model limitsA limit that applies to one model sits under the week it belongs to, thinner and indented. It shares the week’s scale and reset, so it shows a figure and no second countdown.
Even paceThe white tick is where usage would be at an even pace. Weekly rows share one window, so their ticks join into one line. A bar filled past the line is ahead of pace.
RingsThe outer ring is the week and the inner ring is the 5-hour session. The figure is the account’s tightest limit.
One limitOpenAI has no 5-hour limit on this plan, so its group is one bar and says so.
Two budgetsGemini keeps one budget for its own models and another for Claude and GPT models. Each is its own group.

Full window

Grouped by account like the panel, with the usage-against-pace chart in Limits, where there is room for it. Settings opens on the menu bar editor: drag blocks in the preview to rearrange them. Limits is a list on the left and one limit in detail on the right, so it reads the same with one row or ten. The sidebar, range switcher, limit rows, model rows and settings all work; the content area scrolls.

First-run setup

Seven steps for someone who has just downloaded the app. Steps in the rail are clickable, and “Allow…” and “Test connections” change what the step shows.

Notification and states

Two notifications as they arrive, then one limit row in each state it can be in, with the menu bar item it produces, and the panel before anything is connected.

Sample data. Model prices, plan limits, machine figures and session names are invented for the mockups and are consistent with each other: totals are sums of the rows beneath them, and costs are tokens multiplied by the prices in Settings.
